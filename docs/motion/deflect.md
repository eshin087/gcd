# Card: the perfect deflect (the owner's reference footage, 30 fps reads)

Frames: f0 = steel meets steel.
- f1-f2 (0.03-0.07 s): a round hot-orange bloom at the contact, about a torso across, with one thin
  horizontal streak through it. Hottest at f1. The background is never tinted; the bloom is local.
- f3-f5 (0.10-0.17 s): the bloom swells a little and thins; individual sparks show inside it.
- f6-f9 (0.20-0.30 s): bloom gone; tens of tiny sparks (dots, short streaks) spread one to two body
  widths, fall under gravity, orange to red. Stragglers live to ~0.5 s.
- Chain rhythm: contacts 0.30-0.35 s apart in a fast string (13.47, 13.80, 14.33 ...). Each contact
  is its own bloom; the sparks of the last still falling when the next blooms, so a chain BUILDS a
  cloud of falling embers round the two fighters.
- The string's last deflect is the same moment, only bigger (15.83-16.0: the bloom twice the size,
  more sparks, a longer fall). Then the attacker's posture breaks and he reels; the defender stays
  planted. No teleport, no leap.
- Camera: no cut, no zoom in a chain; a small shake per contact.

## Ours (what we built from it)
The bloom is the fire field's heat at the contact (a round hot core with one thin horizontal streak), the
sparks are glyph particles: tens of tiny letters, orange to red, falling under gravity, stragglers to 0.5 s.
A chain builds an ember cloud round the pair because each bloom is its own and the last sparks still fall.
No background tint, no camera cut (`docs/MOTION.md` rule 1). Shipped as the perfect parry's own streak (PR 145).
